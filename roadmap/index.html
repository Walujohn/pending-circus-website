Roadmap

Two purposes, one business. Floor: $20k/year in paid bookings as proof of professional legitimacy. Ceiling: an artistic resume that opens doors to ensemble work, festivals, teams, immersive companies, and original creative projects.

1. Tighten the website and socials

Lead with photos and short clips. Cut the copy. Show core offerings clearly: mirror suit, lollipop, duo dance, movement arts and character themed entertainment, etc. Real event media as it comes in. The site should make sense to a corporate planner and to an artistic director who lands there. Start with one tone; split later if needed.

2. Book gigs, capture proof, choose with intent

Prioritize paid bookings. Hire a photographer or videographer for any gig worth documenting. Apply a second filter on what to say yes to: does it pay, build the artist resume, or both? Most should do both. A weird gallery opening at $300 with great footage can outweigh a $600 corporate logo job. Pay the bills, but stay conscious. Organize footage by act and by tone.

3. Build the media package

Start with one reel, 60–90 seconds, that leans slightly toward taste over polish — the version that books corporate gigs but doesn't confuse us in front of a curator. A simple lookbook PDF. A logo. Refresh the site every month or two. Once we have enough range in the footage, split into two reels: one for corporate clients, one with no rules and all risk.

4. Outreach — warm, cold, and artistic

Three lanes. Warm: coffee chats and honest feedback with industry friends. Cold: event agencies, corporate planners, brand activation shops, wedding planners in Atlanta and the Southeast — the booking pipeline. Artistic: festivals, immersive companies, casting calls, residencies we'd want on the resume. The artistic lane moves slower and may not pay for a long time. That's fine. One good agency relationship outproduces a year of cold outreach; one good artistic connection can change the trajectory entirely.

5. Run the business cleanly

Track income, expenses, taxes, renewals, insurance. Cloud folder for documents. Budget for apparatus, costume repair, transport. Keep training — so the work keeps deepening. Attend one industry con and one artistic-circus event per year when possible (HAuNTcon, IAAPA, Festival Mondial du Cirque de Demain, American Circus Educators). Combine con trips with Airbnb location scouting when the geography lines up.
